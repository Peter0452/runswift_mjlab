K1 kick loop on the real robot vs sim (2026-10-09)#

First real-robot recordings of the kick loop with the step recorder, and what they say about the gap to simulation.

Robot: K1 #291. Other robots may behave differently (motor wear, firmware, calibration); treat hardware-level findings (loss of motor authority, enable flickers, knee torque profile) as 291-specific until another robot shows the same. The user reports that 291’s vision is mostly correct.

Why the robot went on and off (run 1122)#

Four different things, only one of them the policy:

  1. One real dynamic fall (6.2 s). It happened while walking, 3.9 m from the ball: the left knee hit −74 Nm (B-Human clips at 60), impact 104 m/s², knee speed 12 rad/s, and the robot fell backwards. The run used a turn-rate cap of 2.0, outside the trained 1.5.

  2. “Zombie” periods after a fall (17–37 s, 51–60 s, 84–103 s). After a fall the runner sets damping mode. Once someone picks the robot up and it tilts back under 70°, the runner treats it as not fallen and keeps running the policy, but never re-enters custom mode. Commands are ignored: the joints sit about 0.22 rad from their targets at about 1.3 Nm, and the knees read a constant 0.10 rad. The fall flag flips on and off as the limp robot is moved through 70°.

  3. Enable flickers (39.7, 60.0, 94.0, 166.8 s). /nubots_walk/enable drops for 0.3–0.6 s. During the drop nothing commands the joints, and the rising edge does ChangeMode(kCustom) and resets the policy mid-step. The 39.7 s flicker was followed by a fall at 40.6 s.

  4. External loss of motor authority while upright (1120 at 23.3 s; 1122 at 76.8 s and 174.5 s). The runner was still publishing and enabled, but the knee drifted 1.5 rad from its target at only about 6 Nm (kp 70 would give about 100 Nm). The robot then tipped over slowly. Joint speeds (5–9 rad/s) and torques (26–41 Nm) beforehand were normal. This happened near the ball (0.4–0.8 m). The cause is outside the runner and not identifiable from this data: Booster firmware safety, the remote, or another node on /joint_ctrl.

The later “falls” at 81, 178 and 197 s are consequences of 2 and 4.

FOV gate on the real robot. The rejected “detections” sit at 84–115° bearing, 0.3–1.1 m away, with the head yawed at most ±49°. The camera (±35° horizontal half field of view) cannot see there, so the ball topic sometimes carries a filtered or remembered estimate, not a raw detection. The gate rejects them, as training expects.

Timing. 50 Hz loop, state-to-publish 3.2 ms p50 and 18–25 ms p99, 13–37 steps more than 30 ms apart.

What this means for the sim#

  • Cadence: the real robot steps about 20 % slower than sim with the same policy, so either actuation is slower than modelled (tracking error is higher too) or the swing is limited. Candidates to calibrate: actuator delay range (training 0–40 ms; measured lag here is about 80 ms including the PD response, the same as sim, so check the armature and damping of the swing joints first) and PD gains on the hardware.

  • Knee load: real knees carry more torque and the hips less than in sim, a sign of a different posture or contact; consistent with the lower forward trunk pitch.

  • Impacts: the median footfall is softer on the real robot, but the worst 10 % are harder than anything in sim. That tail is what makes the steps loud. Contact stiffness and damping of the real foot and floor are not captured.

  • Vision: detections arrive at about 16 Hz, not every step, and the ball is lost 2–13 % of the time. The user reports 291’s vision is mostly correct, so this is how the vision stack behaves rather than an error; low priority. If anything, match the training detection rate (dropout) to about 16 Hz.

Runner fixes indicated by the data#

  1. Latch falls: once fallen, stop publishing and stay in damping until an explicit re-enable while upright, then re-enter custom mode and reset.

  2. Debounce enable: ignore drops under about 0.5 s and do not reset the policy on a flicker; find why the publisher drops.

  3. Detect lost motor authority: mean |q − target| above about 0.4 rad with |τ| far below kp × error for more than 0.2 s, then stop and log.

  4. Record every loop iteration (including disabled and fallen), the runner’s mode decisions, the Booster robot mode, motor error codes and temperatures, and battery voltage.

  5. Caps: wz 2.0 is outside training; use 1.5, 1.5, 1.0 with the soft start (cap_ramp_s), which scored best in the official runswift goal grid in sim.

    --model-path ../kick_loop_models/k1_kick_loop_h022_c2_18450.onnx \
    --kick-speed-limits 1.5,1.5,2.0 --ball-xy 3.5,0.5 --headless --duration 40 \
    --kick-record ../runs/sim_ref
